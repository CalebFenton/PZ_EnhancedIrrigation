# Enhanced Irrigation: player's guide

The build menu and the crafting window list every recipe and what it takes, and the mod's menus and tooltips tell you what you're looking at: the litres waiting in a filter, a seal's wear, whether a pump has power. This guide covers the rest: what each thing is for, how one setup leads to the next, and the rules that decide whether water gets where you want it. Figures assume the default sandbox settings. Reach, costs, times and skill levels are exact; rates are rounded.

## How water works

Every drop follows two rules, and both are about height.

- **Pressure.** A tap, a hole in a garden line or a plumbed sink runs only from a tank at least a level above it. A barrel on the ground waters nothing by gravity.
- **Filling.** Water that only has to fill something, such as another tank, a filter or a boiler, needs its source no lower than what it fills. Water never runs uphill.

Pumps get around both: they lift water from a well, a barrel or a river up to the height they're rated for.

### Levels

A tank's level is the floor it stands on, plus one if it's on a Tank Stand.

| Tank | Level |
|---|---|
| A barrel on the ground | 0 |
| A barrel on a Tank Stand, or on a floor a storey up | 1 |
| A barrel on a Tank Stand a storey up | 2 |

A higher tank pushes harder: two levels above the line run about 1.4 times as fast as one, three about 1.7 times.

### What counts as a tank

Any of vanilla's rain barrels and rain collectors, an amphora, or a barrel on a Tank Stand, with its lid open or shut. Wells, troughs, sinks and toilets aren't tanks, though a pump draws from a well or a trough. A tank fills from rain through its open lid, from vanilla's pour, from a tank plumbed above it, from a pump, or from a filter's outlet.

### Taint

Water keeps whatever taint it had, all the way down the pipe. Rain comes in tainted, and vanilla's rule holds: a container with any tainted water in it is tainted, so one bucket of river water spoils a full tank. Most homesteads end up with two tanks: a big raw one for the garden, the animals and the toilet, and a small clean one with its lid shut, filled only through a filter or a boiler, for the kitchen.

That goes for vanilla's rooftop barrel too. Plumbed with vanilla's **Plumb**, it now gives its water as it is, tainted, instead of purifying it. Sandbox: **Plumbing keeps taint**.

### Water and plants

The mod uses vanilla's exchange rate: 1 L is 50 points of a plant's water, a plant holds 100, and it uses about 5 a day. A dry plant takes 2 L to fill. Rain and watering cans work exactly as in vanilla.

## Your first garden line

You need a barrel a level up, pipe and its joints, something to make holes, and a Pipe Wrench.

1. Raise a barrel: build a **Tank Stand** and **Set Barrel on Stand** with an empty rain barrel, or build a barrel on a deck or floor a storey up. Open its lid for rain, or pour water in.
2. Carrying pipe, right-click the ground beside the barrel and pick the pipe under **Lay Line**. The cursor stays up, so click out a run along the bed, on the plants' own squares.
3. **Drill Holes** in each tile under a plant.
4. **Fit Tap** on a tile beside the raised barrel.
5. Stand at the tap and **Water from Tank**. Every hole runs and the soil darkens under it until you stop or walk off.

A tank one level up gives each hole about 1 L an hour. A dry plant fills in about 2 game hours, and about 6 minutes at the tap a day keeps a bed watered. Holding the tap costs no endurance.

## Moving up

Each step buys back time, and costs parts, skill or power:

- **A cistern.** Plumb barrels on one level together and they share their water as one big tank.
- **Running taps.** Plumb a sink, a bath, a toilet or a washing machine to a tank, and it works again after the shutoff.
- **Clean water.** A Filter Crock for drinking water without plumbing, a Barrel Filter for a household, then a Brick Filter or a boiler for more.
- **Lifting.** A hand pump on a well, a barrel or a river fills the high tank, or drives a garden line straight. A Pump House does the same on electric power.
- **Letting it run.** A Wind-up Valve shuts the tap for you; a Timer Valve or a Sprinkler Timer opens it every day. Lawn sprinklers on a pumped line water a whole patch at once.

### Ways to clean water

| Method | Clean water | What it asks of you |
|---|---|---|
| Filter Crock | About 10 L a day | Scrub it after 300 L; keep it from a hard frost while it holds water |
| Barrel Filter | About 100 L a day, once ripe | Three days to ripen; water every day or two to keep it alive; clean it after 2000 L |
| Brick Filter | About 400 L a day, once ripe | The same, cleaned after 8000 L |
| Boiler | About 30 L an hour while hot | Fuel; the drum wears out, and burns through fast if it runs dry |

### Ways to lift water

| Pump | Lifts | Into a tank up to | Runs on | Wear |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wooden Hand Pump | About 6 L a minute | A level above it | Your endurance; it squeals | The seal, and slowly the wood |
| Iron Hand Pump | About 8 L a minute | Two levels above it | Your endurance | The seal, and very slowly the iron |
| Pump House, Appliance Pump | About 6 L a minute | A level above it | Power | About 1% an hour running |
| Pump House, Utility Pump | About 20 L a minute | Two levels above it | Power | About 0.5% an hour running |

## Tanks and stands

A **Tank Stand** holds one rain barrel a level up. It takes the round 600 L barrel, with or without a tarp, lid open or shut; the square rain collector and the amphora don't fit. Vanilla only lets you pick a barrel up empty, so it goes up empty and fills where it stands. The big rain barrels on the map are the round kind; the small ones are square collectors.

- **Set Barrel on Stand** takes 5 minutes of heavy work, carrying the barrel.
- **Take Barrel Down** takes 5 minutes, once the barrel is empty, and gives back vanilla's barrel.
- An empty stand can't be picked up; disassembling it gives back some planks and nails. A barrel on a stand can be neither picked up nor disassembled, so take it down first.

A raised barrel catches rain like vanilla's while its lid is open. Stands aren't the only way up: a barrel built on a deck or an upstairs floor is a level up too, and one on a stand up there is two.

## Plumbing tanks together

**Plumb to Tank**, from a tank's menu, lists the tanks in reach and the pipe you carry.

- **Reach.** Up to 10 tiles, counted straight across and rounded up, plus 2 tiles for each storey between the two tanks. One storey apart leaves 8 tiles across.
- **Pipe.** One length for every 3 tiles, rounded up, so a run takes at most 4. Plastic needs one use of glue, epoxy or duct tape a length; clay takes 2 segments a length and one use of cement or concrete; iron and lead need only the Pipe Wrench, which you keep.
- **Time.** 10 minutes, plus 3 a tile: the longest run takes 40.

Water moves every ten minutes while both tanks are loaded, at most 360 L an hour a link. Tanks on the same level share until each is filled to the same fraction. Between levels, the higher tank fills the lower one until it's full or the higher one is empty, so plumbing a raised barrel to one on the ground empties the raised one. Taint goes with the water.

A tank can be plumbed to several others, so a deck of barrels on one level acts as one cistern.

The pipe runs out of sight, through walls and under floors, and never freezes or wears. It stays in the ground when a tank leaves its end, and a tank placed on that square is connected again. **Unplumb** from either tank, or from the bare floor where a tank stood, takes 10 minutes with the wrench and gives every length back in the condition it went in; the joints are used up.

## Sinks, baths, toilets and washers

Right-click a sink, a bath, a shower, a toilet or a washing machine for **Plumb to Tank**: the same reach, pipe, time and learning as plumbing two tanks. The tank has to stand at least a level above the fixture. Each fixture takes one tank, and a tank can feed many fixtures, so the toilet can run off the raw tank while the kitchen sink runs off the clean one. The fixture doesn't need to be in a room, so a cabin you built yourself works.

- **Its water.** Where no town water reaches it, a plumbed fixture holds its own: 20 L for a sink, toilet, shower or washer, 100 L for a bath. It fills from the tank, then refills by up to 20 L every ten minutes while the tank has water and the height. Drink, fill, wash and the washer use vanilla's own actions on it, with vanilla's taint warning.
- **The town's water wins while it runs.** Until the shutoff, a plumbed fixture in a room that nobody has moved gives clean town water and leaves the tank alone, then switches to the tank within ten minutes of the shutoff, so plumbing early costs nothing. A fixture outside any room, such as one in a cabin you built, or one you carried in, draws from its tank at once. The water vanilla leaves in a fixture's pipes after the shutoff is set aside while it's plumbed, and comes back when you unplumb it.
- **Vanilla's Plumb** still works on a fixture in a room under a barrel one storey up, without learning and without pipe. It makes the same connection, so the barrel's taint comes through.
- **A washing machine** keeps its **Turn On** switch. It needs power, and draws about a litre a minute while it runs, which the tank keeps up with.

**Unplumb** from the fixture, the tank or the bare floor where the tank stood gives every length back; vanilla's Plumb laid none.

## Garden lines

### Laying a line

Right-click the ground while carrying pipe and pick the kind under **Lay Line**; each tile takes 3 minutes and no skill.

| Pipe | A tile takes | Freezes |
|---|---|---|
| Plastic | 1 pipe and one use of glue, epoxy or duct tape | Seldom |
| Iron | 1 pipe, joined with a Pipe Wrench | Now and then |
| Lead | 1 pipe, joined with a Pipe Wrench | Seldom |
| Clay | 2 segments and one use of cement or concrete | Most often |

Wood glue and adhesive tape don't hold water.

The first tile goes in the 3×3 around a tank on the same floor or one floor up, or in the 3×3 around a pump for the pump to feed. After that each tile goes next to the line. A line stays on one floor and needs open ground or floor, not water or a square furniture blocks. Walls and windows split it; doorways, fences and low walls don't, so a line can follow you into a greenhouse.

### Holes

A hole waters only the plant on its own tile, so lay the line on the plants' squares. **Drill Holes** takes 2 minutes a tile, and uses the first tool you carry that gets through the pipe:

| Tool | Gets through |
|---|---|
| Hand Drill | Any pipe |
| Auger Drill | Anything but iron |
| Awl, Ice Pick, Handiknife, Multitool, small punch set | Plastic and lead |
| Stone Drill | Anything but iron |
| A hammer and a nail, which is used up | Anything but iron |

The Stone Drill and the hammer punch rather than cut, so 1 hole in 5 cracks plastic or clay, and the menu warns you.

### The tap

**Fit Tap** takes 10 minutes, a Pipe Wrench, one more length of the line's pipe and its joint. It goes on a line tile with a tank at least a level above it, on or beside the tile or on the floor above. A line takes one tap or one pump's outlet, never both, and you can't lay a tile that would join two lines that each have one.

Stand at the tap and **Water from Tank**: every hole on the line runs until you stop, walk off, or the tank runs dry or loses its height. Only one person can hold a tap at a time. Each time water runs, the tap draws from the highest tank in its reach that holds water, the fullest if two are equally high, and tanks plumbed to that one refill it every ten minutes.

### How much it gives

Each hole gives about 1 L an hour from a tank one level up, more from higher ones. Every hole runs at once, so 24 holes draw 24 L an hour, and a tank that can't keep up gives every hole the same share. The ground under a working hole shows wet for a few hours, longer while the soil is soaked.

## Too much water

Water a hole gives past what its plant can take soaks the soil, and so does everything a hole over bare ground gives. Rain never soaks the soil, so vanilla farming is unchanged.

- **Drying.** Soil dries 10 points a day outdoors and 5 indoors.
- **Seeing it.** From Agriculture 2, the plant info window shows a **Soil** line: Damp, Soaked, then Waterlogged.
- **Root rot.** While the soil is waterlogged, the plant loses health about as fast as a parched one. From a tank one level up, an hour at the tap past full keeps a plant waterlogged for about 2 days outdoors and 4 indoors; two hours past full, a week outdoors or two indoors, which can kill a weak plant. A pumped line soaks twice as fast, so an hour past full there already means a week.
- **Mixed beds.** Every hole runs together, so topping up one thirsty plant soaks every full plant on the line. Plant a line's beds at the same time, or water a newcomer by hand.

## Frost and repairs

Once a tap has run, its line stays full of water until you drain it.

- **Freezing.** At -7 °C or colder, a full tile may split. A heated room keeps its tiles safe while you're nearby, but once you leave the area the line freezes as if it were outdoors. Left full outdoors all winter, a 24-tile line can expect about 3 cracks in clay, 1 or 2 in iron, and seldom one in lead or plastic.
- **Drain Line**, from any tile, takes 2 minutes plus 1 for every 5 tiles, empties the whole line and wets the spot where you opened it. It's greyed out while a pump or its tap's valve runs the line.
- **Cracks** leak about 3 L an hour while the tap runs, more from a higher tank and two or three times that on a pumped line, onto the ground where no plant gets it. **Patch Crack** takes 3 minutes and one use of glue, epoxy or duct tape, on any pipe.
- **Take Up Pipe** takes 3 minutes and gives each length back as it went in; laid pipe doesn't wear. A cracked length is lost, and the joints are used up. A sledgehammer also removes a tile, but you lose the pipe.

## Clean water

All of these turn tainted water into clean water completely or not at all, as vanilla does. What sets them apart is how fast they are, what they cost, and how much looking after they need. **Pour In**, from the menu of any of them, takes any container of water in your main inventory, a minute for every 4 L, and the container keeps what doesn't fit. The clean side, a filter's basin, the crock's bucket or the boiler's tap, works with vanilla's drink, fill and empty.

### Sand filters

A **Barrel Filter** or a **Brick Filter** is a slow sand filter: what cleans the water is the living film on top of the sand, so you keep it alive.

- **How fast.** Raw water waits on top of the sand and seeps into the basin below: about 4 L an hour through a Barrel Filter and 16 through a Brick Filter. The top and the basin each hold 50 L in a barrel and 200 L in brick, and once the basin is full the water waits on top. The menu's first line says how much is waiting and how the sand is.
- **Ripening.** A new filter passes water as tainted as it came in for its first 3 days of use, counted only while water stands on the sand, then clean. Check the basin with vanilla's tooltip, and empty it before you plumb its outlet to a clean tank, or the first batch taints the tank.
- **Keeping it alive.** Two days with nothing on the sand kill the film, and it ripens again from scratch. So does a hard freeze (-7 °C or colder) while water stands on the sand. An empty filter, or one in a heated room, rides out the frost.
- **Clogging.** After 2000 L through a Barrel Filter, or 8000 L through a Brick Filter, it slows to a tenth and its menu says **Clogged**. **Clean Filter** takes 60 minutes by hand, or 30 with a shovel, a garden trowel or a mason's trowel, and the filter ripens again for a day after.

### Filter Crock

A porous clay pot standing in a bucket: clean water without any plumbing. Shape it at a pottery wheel, fire it at a kiln, then set it up from the build menu with an empty bucket.

- **Pour In** up to 8 L of raw water. It seeps through the clay into the bucket, clean however tainted it went in, at about 10 L a day. Nothing to ripen, nothing to keep alive. Whatever bucket it stands in fills to 10 L, and then the water waits in the crock.
- **Clogging.** After 300 L it slows to a tenth and says **Clogged**; **Scrub Crock** takes 15 minutes by hand and brings it straight back.
- **Frost.** A hard freeze with any water in the crock cracks it: the water runs out, it passes nothing more, Pour In and Scrub Crock are refused and its menu says **cracked**. An empty crock, or one in a heated room, is safe.
- **Take Down** takes 2 minutes and gives back the bucket with its water, and the crock unless it's cracked. Raw water still in the crock spills.

### Boiler

A Metal Drum over a firebox, stone or welded; both work the same. It's a vanilla fire, like the Barrel Oven: its menu has vanilla's fire options for fuel, **Light Fire** and **Put out Fire**, and it burns, lights and warms like one. It goes outdoors with no wall on its square or the eight around it, and like vanilla's fireplaces it never spreads fire.

- **How it works.** Raw water waits in the drum, up to 200 L. Lit, the drum is hot after an hour, and while hot it passes about 30 L an hour into its tap, clean, until the tap holds 200 L. Put out after a couple of hours of fire, it stays hot about half an hour more. A boiler that never got hot passes nothing. The menu says whether the drum is cold, heating or hot.
- **Wear.** A drum lasts about 200 hours of fire with water in it, but 10 hours of fire with nothing in the drum burn it through. Burnt through, it holds and passes nothing, its inlet draws nothing and Pour In is refused until you patch it.
- **Patch Drum** takes Welding 2, a small steel sheet, 2 uses of a welding torch and 1 of welding rods, and a welder mask; 30 minutes. Each patch brings it back 10% less than the last, to 90%, then 80%, and it's refused while the drum is sounder than the patch would leave it. Once there's nothing left to patch, build a new boiler.

### Plumbing a filter or a boiler

A filter or a boiler takes one inlet and one outlet, to two different tanks, with Plumb to Tank's reach, pipe, time and learning.

- **Plumb Inlet**, to a tank standing at least level with it, tops it up every ten minutes, as long as the tank holds nothing but water. Taint goes with it, which is the point.
- **Plumb Outlet**, to a tank standing no higher than it, empties the basin into the tank every ten minutes. Use a lidded tank so rain can't taint it.

The classic chain: a raw tank on a platform, the filter beside it on the same level, the clean tank beside that, and the sinks below.

## Pumps

A hand pump or a Pump House draws from the first of these it finds:

1. A well beside it, the stone well or the forest's old hand pump: clean and endless.
2. The fullest rain barrel, rain collector, amphora or water trough beside it, its water as it is.
3. For a pump on the ground, the nearest river or pond within 3 tiles, which its hose reaches: endless, and always tainted.

Beside means the 8 squares around it. Build it anywhere; it says **No water to draw** until it has a source. The menu's first line names the source, the pump's condition and what it fills.

**Plumb Outlet**, from the pump's menu, goes to one tank or one garden line in reach, with Plumb to Tank's reach, pipe and learning. The pump decides how high the tank may stand (see [Ways to lift water](#ways-to-lift-water)); a tank too high is greyed out saying how high the pump lifts. A pump never fills its own source. A line it feeds runs whenever the pump does, as if its tap were held.

### Hand pumps

Stand at the pump and **Pump**; only one person can work a pump at a time. A 600 L barrel takes about 100 minutes from a wooden pump and 75 from an iron one. It's hard work, about 60% of your endurance an hour, less what standing still gives back. It stops by itself when the tank is full, the source runs dry, the seal or the pump wears out, or you're exhausted. Zombies hear it too: a wooden pump's squeal carries about 15 tiles, an iron pump's about 8.

| Seal | Lifts before it's worn through |
|---|---|
| Leather Strips | 3000 L |
| Tire Piece | 2000 L |
| Tarp Piece | 1200 L |
| A Plunger's cup | 600 L |

**Replace Seal** takes 10 minutes and a screwdriver, and throws the old seal away. The Plunger only goes in this way. The pump itself wears 1% every 300 L in wood and every 1500 L in iron, about 30,000 L and 150,000 L in all. Worn out, it lifts nothing: knock it down with a sledgehammer and build another.

### Pump Houses

A Pump House is built empty. **Fit Pump** puts in an Appliance Pump or a Utility Pump as worn as it is (15 minutes, a screwdriver); **Take Out Pump** gives it back (10 minutes). **Turn On** and **Turn Off** take a minute. Switched on, it runs every ten minutes while it has power. The water stops when the tank is full; the pump doesn't. You hear its motor, and so do zombies: about 10 tiles off for an Appliance Pump and 20 for a Utility Pump.

**Power** comes from the first of:

- **The grid**, free until the shutoff, but only in a room or right beside one, as for a washer: never out in the open.
- **A running generator in reach**, which burns fuel for the pump at vanilla's rate, as much as a washer for an Appliance Pump and about twice that for a Utility Pump. When the generator runs dry it shuts off; the house stays switched on, says **no power**, and runs again once the generator is refuelled and started.
- **A Renewable Energy battery bank**, if you play with that mod. It bills the pump as an appliance at the same rate; a Utility Pump alone is more than a plug-in inverter carries.

**Wear.** Running with nothing to draw wears the pump ten times as fast. At 0% it burns out and switches off. **Repair Pump** (Electricity 2; a screwdriver, Scrap Electronics and a Tire Piece; 30 minutes) brings it back to 10% below its last ceiling, and is refused while the pump is better than that.

**Getting a pump.** **Salvage Pump** on a washing machine, washer-dryer or dishwasher (Electricity 2, a screwdriver and pliers, 30 minutes) gives an Appliance Pump at 60 to 100% and a Water Valve, and leaves the appliance where it stands, never to run again, even moved. Utility Pumps are found, or made from a Blower Fan with **Make Utility Pump**, which comes out at 80%, its ceiling for good.

## Sprinklers

A Lawn Sprinkler on a garden line waters a whole patch without a hole drilled, but only on a line a pump drives, while the pump runs: a tank's height can't turn it.

- **Set Sprinkler** from a line tile's menu, carrying the sprinkler and a Pipe Wrench: 5 minutes. One to a tile, and not on the tap's tile. **Take Sprinkler** (3 minutes) gives it back as it went on.
- **Where it waters.** A patch 5 squares long and 3 wide, centred on it. The long side runs north to south where the line runs or ends that way at its tile, and east to west at a corner, a junction or anywhere else. Each square gets about 1.8 L an hour: a dry bed fills in about an hour, and about 3 minutes a day keeps it level.
- **Its cost.** It asks the line for about 1 L a minute, half of it lost to the air and the paths, which any pump lifts with plenty to spare for the line's holes. The soil it soaks rots roots just as a line's does, and where two patches overlap, both soak the same soil.
- **Make Pipe Sprinkler** turns an iron pipe and an empty gardening spray can into a sprinkler at 80%. Sprinklers don't wear.

A pumped line pushes harder than a tank, too: each hole gives about 2 L an hour from a hand pump or an Appliance Pump, and 3 from a Utility Pump.

## Valves and timers

Fit a valve to a tap and it runs the tap for you, exactly as if you held it. **Fit Valve** (5 minutes) and **Take Off Valve** (3 minutes) need a Pipe Wrench; one valve to a tap. None of them wear out.

| Valve | What it does | Power |
|---|---|---|
| Wind-up Valve | Runs the tap for the minutes you wind, then shuts | None |
| Timer Valve | Runs it every day at the hour you set, for the minutes you set | Needs it |
| Sprinkler Timer | The same as a Timer Valve, found rather than made | Needs it |

- **Wind Valve...** opens the knob, the oven timer's: 1 to 5 minutes, 10 to 30 in fives, then 40, 50, 60, 90 or 120. **Wind** sets it running; winding to 0 shuts it.
- **Set Timer...** opens the timer's panel: the hour it starts and the same knob for how long; 0 never runs.
- **How long.** A tank one level up gives each hole a little under a point of plant water a minute, so about 6 minutes a day keeps a bed level. A higher tank needs less. Hold the tap and watch the soil to judge it, and remember a timer knows nothing of the rain.
- **Power.** A timer needs its tap's square powered, as a washer does: the grid in or beside a room, a generator in reach, or a Renewable Energy bank. It draws too little to bill. Without power it doesn't open.
- **On a Pump House.** **Fit Timer** (5 minutes, a screwdriver) puts a Timer Valve or a Sprinkler Timer between the house and its power. Leave the house switched off and it runs in the timer's window each day; switched on, it runs all the time, whatever the timer says. A Wind-up Valve doesn't fit.

While a valve runs, the tap's menu says **water running**. If someone holds the tap when a timer would open, their watering counts instead.

## Learning and crafting

### *Cisterns for Kentucky*

A University of Kentucky extension pamphlet on keeping and moving farm water. It teaches the Tank Stand, Plumb to Tank (for tanks, fixtures, filters, boilers and pumps), both hand pumps, both Pump Houses and both sand filters. Reading it eases boredom and stress, as vanilla's magazines do.

### Other ways to learn

Every recipe but the Pump Houses is also learned by reaching a skill level, usually above what using it needs, so the pamphlet gets you there sooner. The Pump Houses come only from the pamphlet or from researching a pump.

| Skill | Teaches |
|---|---|
| Carpentry | Plumb to Tank and the Wooden Hand Pump at 4, the Tank Stand at 5, the Barrel Filter at 6 |
| Welding | Plumb to Tank at 3, Make Pipe Sprinkler at 4, the Iron Hand Pump and the welded boiler at 5 |
| Masonry | The stone boiler at 4, the Brick Filter at 5 |
| Pottery | The Filter Crock, shaping, firing and setting it up, at 5 |
| Electricity | Salvage Pump at 2, Make Timer Valve at 4, Make Utility Pump at 6 |

Researching an item teaches what it's part of, from about two thirds of the recipe's skill:

- A Ceramic Jar or a Filter Crock: the Filter Crock.
- A Metal Drum: the boilers you have the skill for.
- An Appliance Pump or a Utility Pump: the Pump Houses, and a Utility Pump Make Utility Pump too.
- A Lawn Sprinkler: Make Pipe Sprinkler.
- A Water Valve or a Sprinkler Timer: Make Timer Valve.

A character who already has a level when the mod is added learns its recipes on entering the world.

### What needs no learning

Vanilla's Plumb, laying lines, drilling holes, fitting taps, watering, patching, draining and taking up pipe, sprinklers, Pour In, cleaning and scrubbing, Patch Drum, pumping and seals, fitting and repairing pumps, Turn On and Off, every valve and timer action, and Make Wind-up Valve.

### What a recipe needs around it

Builds and crafts need light to see by, as vanilla's do. The made parts, Make Utility Pump, Make Wind-up Valve and Make Timer Valve, need a table or counter; the crock a pottery wheel, then a kiln. Most give XP in their skill.

### What you can't make

Appliance Pumps only come out of appliances, and Water Valves mostly do. Sprinkler Timers are only found. A made Utility Pump or Lawn Sprinkler never gets better than 80%.

## When something isn't working

### What the menu says

| It says | What to do |
|---|---|
| The tank must stand a storey above the line | Raise the tank, or feed the line from a pump instead |
| Start a line beside a tank or a pump | Lay the first tile in the 3×3 around one |
| This would join two lines that each have a tap or a pump | Leave a gap between them |
| Needs a tool that pierces this pipe | Bring a Hand Drill for iron, or another tool from [Holes](#holes) |
| Someone is already at this tap | Wait for them to finish |
| The tap's valve is running it | Wait for it to shut, or **Take Off Valve** |
| ripening, *N* hours to go | Keep water on the sand; empty the basin before plumbing its outlet |
| dry for *N* hours | Pour water in before two days pass, or it ripens again from scratch |
| Clogged | **Clean Filter** or **Scrub Crock** |
| cold, heating | Light the boiler and give it an hour |
| burnt through | **Patch Drum**; once it's too patched to take another, build a new boiler |
| cracked | Take the crock down and fire a new one |
| No water to draw | Move the pump beside a well, a barrel or a trough, or within 3 tiles of a river |
| seal worn out | **Replace Seal** |
| burnt out | **Repair Pump**, or fit another |
| no power | Refuel and start the generator, or bring one in reach; before the shutoff, the grid reaches a Pump House only in or beside a room |
| The tank it fills is full | Use the water, or plumb the tank on to another |

### Other troubles

- **Plants sicken though they're watered**: the soil is waterlogged. Check the **Soil** line, and water less, or plant the beds together.
- **Water leaks onto the ground**: a tile is cracked. **Patch Crack**.
- **A sprinkler does nothing**: it needs a pump driving the line, running.
- **A sink's water is tainted**: its tank is. Plumb it to a clean tank a filter or a boiler fills.
- **A timer didn't open**: it had no power, or someone was holding the tap.
- **A recipe is greyed out with everything in hand**: you need more light, or a table.

## While you're away

The world keeps time while you're out of range, but not everything works without you.

- **Lines.** Soaked soil keeps drying, and a full line can still freeze, indoors or out. A tap you held stops when you leave.
- **Links between tanks** wait until both tanks are loaded.
- **Filters, crocks, boilers, Pump Houses and valves** wait, then catch up when you come back, a week at most: a filter drains and dries, a Pump House pumps as long as its power lasted, and a timer runs every time it would have. A plumbed filter or boiler waits for its inlet and outlet tanks to be in range before catching up.
- **Power** while away is judged when you come back. A Pump House counts the grid's hours up to the shutoff, a generator's as far as its fuel went, and none if it has no power when you return. A timer counts every hour if it has power when you return, or the grid's up to the shutoff if the grid powered it when you left.

## Moving things and your save

- **Moving things.** A Tank Stand can't be moved: take its barrel down, empty, and disassemble the stand. **Take Up Pipe**, **Unplumb**, **Take Sprinkler**, **Take Off Valve**, **Take Out Pump** and **Take Down** give back what went in, except the joints and anything cracked. Moving a tank leaves its buried pipe behind, ready for a tank on that square.
- **Adding to a save.** The pamphlet and the found parts turn up only in containers nobody has opened yet. Barrels already in your world count as tanks straight away, and every washer and dishwasher can be salvaged. With **Plumbing keeps taint** on, a fixture vanilla plumbed before you added the mod changes over the first time its area loads.
- **Removing from a save.** What the mod built into your world goes with it.

## Where to look

Think about who in 1993 Kentucky kept water moving: farmers and gardeners, plumbers, hardware stores and the county extension office. Rarity is relative to the mod's other items and moves with the loot options.

| What | Who'd have had it | How often |
|---|---|---|
| *Cisterns for Kentucky* | Garden centres most of all, then farms, homesteaders, hardware stores, libraries and post offices | Uncommon: about as often as one of vanilla's farming magazines |
| Utility Pump | Hardware stores' tools, and tool crates at home | Rare |
| Lawn Sprinkler | Garden stores, and the garden kit in garages and sheds | Rare |
| Sprinkler Timer | Garden stores and hardware stores' garden aisles | Rare |
| Water Valve | Plumbing supplies, now and then | Very rare: **Salvage Pump** is the way to get one |
| Pipe | Plumbers, building sites, warehouses and hardware stores | Plastic and lead uncommon, iron common; clay is made at a kiln |
| Pipe Wrench | Plumbers and hardware stores | Uncommon |

Worn pumps and sprinklers sometimes turn up in old tool crates. Every washing machine and dishwasher holds a pump and a valve, at the cost of the appliance.

Nothing is set up for you. Every stand, line, filter and pump in the world is one you build.
